\title{Same Top Fraction, Different Workload:\\
Auditing Eligibility and Capacity in Clinical Risk Evaluation}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Clinical risk scores turn patient rankings into finite worklists for specialist
review, monitoring, or prevention. Yet cross-cohort evaluations often compare
the same top fraction across candidate sets of different sizes, changing both
who can be ranked and how many people receive service. We introduce a
decision-aware four-cell audit that keeps the full-population event denominator
fixed and crosses two eligibility rules with two absolute budgets, separating
broader reach from additional capacity. We apply it to frozen birth-record
score vectors for preterm birth, neonatal intensive care admission, and low
birth weight in approximately 3.48 million CDC Natality 2023 records. At the
nominal top 10\% operating point, moving from early-entry eligibility to a
retrospective all-record upper bound reports a 39--42\% relative increase in
captured events while expanding the worklist by one third. Symmetric accounting
allocates about three fifths of the joint gain to added capacity and two fifths
to broader eligibility; at a fixed budget, the eligibility gain is less than
half the cohort-relative estimate. In 22\% of controlled conditions, the
cohort-relative comparison improves while fixed-budget capture declines. The
audit therefore distinguishes population reach from resource expansion and
makes the policy represented by a top-fraction metric explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Clinical risk models increasingly determine which records receive specialist
review, monitoring, outreach, or preventive services
\citep{annarumma2019triage,baltruschat2021worklist,jung2021framework,singh2023resource}.
These workflows are capacity constrained: a model produces a ranking, but an
organization acts on a finite number of cases. Top-$q$ precision, recall, or
event capture therefore appears to connect predictive performance directly to
an operational decision. Prenatal case-management studies, for example,
evaluate the highest-risk 5--15\% because program capacity covers a fraction of
eligible pregnancies \citep{pan2017prenatal}.

The concrete scale of this policy difference is visible in CDC Natality 2023
\citep{cdcNatality}. An early-entry candidate set of approximately 2.60 million
records yields a top-10\% worklist of about 260,000, whereas an all-record set
of approximately 3.48 million yields about 348,000 selections at the same
nominal fraction. The comparison therefore changes both the candidate gate and
the absolute workload. Clinical decision frameworks distinguish predictive
ranking from action under finite resources
\citep{jung2021framework,singh2023resource}: a fixed-capacity service and a
service that expands with candidate volume implement different policies and
require different contrasts.

Prenatal risk triage makes this ambiguity visible. Pregnancies enter care at
different times, and adverse outcomes are concentrated among records with no
documented prenatal care or unknown start timing. An early-entry score ranks
events inside its candidate gate; events outside the gate contribute zero
reachable cases regardless of discrimination. National vital statistics retain both the eligible and
excluded outcomes, making it possible to distinguish retrieval within the gate
from reach across the complete target population.

We audit this distinction on CDC Natality preterm birth, neonatal intensive
care unit (NICU) admission, and low birth weight. Figure~\ref{fig:headline}
previews the result. At the nominal top 10\% operating point, all-record
upper-bound evaluation selects roughly 88,000 additional records per outcome. Population
event capture rises substantially, but about 62\% of the joint increase is
allocated to the larger workload. A controlled mechanism grid then identifies
conditions in which the resource change is large enough to reverse the
fixed-budget conclusion.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An auditable decision estimand.} Population event capture keeps
  every target-population event in the denominator, while a complete
  eligibility-by-budget design exposes the two fixed-budget contrasts and their
  interaction.
  \item \textbf{A national-scale measurement study.} Frozen 2022 scoring
  pipelines applied to CDC 2023 show that the same top 10\% expands workload by
  33.8\% and that capacity receives 61.6--62.2\% of the symmetric allocation
  across three outcomes.
  \item \textbf{A mechanism map and reporting profile.} An 81-condition stress
  test locates sign-reversal regimes, and the resulting checklist makes the
  candidate gate, absolute workload, and event denominator explicit.
\end{enumerate}

Code for the four-cell audit and synthetic experiments, together with the
aggregate artifacts used to generate the reported figures and tables, will be
released upon publication.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Risk ranking under limited resources.}
Prenatal case-management, imaging worklists, and clinical workflow studies
connect risk scores to finite review or treatment capacity
\citep{pan2017prenatal,annarumma2019triage,baltruschat2021worklist,jung2021framework,singh2023resource}.
They motivate operating points such as top 5\%, top 10\%, or a fixed number of
patients and show why use-aligned metrics matter. Calibrated subset selection
similarly constructs shortlists with guarantees inside a supplied candidate
pool \citep{wang2022screening}. These approaches make selection within a
declared pool more reliable, while leaving cross-pool comparisons dependent on
how the shortlist size changes with the pool.

\paragraph{Selective access to clinical data and services.}
Clinical records reflect who enters care, is tested, and receives a documented
label. Informed presence and selection bias can alter both apparent risk and
the population represented by an EHR cohort
\citep{haneuse2016selection,goldstein2016presence,sisk2021informative}; disparate
censorship describes related variation in testing and label availability
\citep{chang2022censorship}. Health-service coverage frameworks separate
service availability from realized coverage \citep{tanahashi1978coverage}.
This literature establishes that the candidate gate is informative, but a
top-fraction comparison adds a second moving quantity because the number served
scales with the observed cohort.

\paragraph{Evaluation validity and decomposition.}
Clinical reporting guidance requires explicit target populations, intended
use, discrimination, and calibration \citep{collins2024tripod}. Symmetric
allocation across factor orderings is established decomposition machinery
\citep{kitagawa1955components,shapley1953value,shorrocks2013decomposition}.
Taken together, these conventions still leave the operational meaning of a metric
implicit when candidate membership and absolute capacity change together. The
case is common and concrete: a percentage-based budget is held numerically
constant across cohorts, its absolute size moves, and the resulting gain is
reported as if it represented one intervention.

\subsection{Target population and scoring study}

We use CDC Natality public-use live-birth records and official field
definitions \citep{cdcNatality,cdcWorksheet}. CDC 2022 supplies model
development with an 80/20 train/validation split; the frozen pipelines generate
the shared score vectors analyzed in CDC 2023. Model fitting and hyperparameter
selection use CDC 2022 exclusively; CDC 2023 is the temporal cohort in which
the audit hypothesis was developed and evaluated. The target population contains
U.S.-resident singleton live births with a known outcome: preterm birth below
37 completed obstetric-estimate weeks ($N=3{,}480{,}382$, 303,001 events), NICU
admission ($N=3{,}476{,}584$, 303,285 events), and birth weight below 2,500 g
($N=3{,}480{,}316$, 243,776 events).

Prenatal-care month defines four mutually exclusive groups: early entry in
months 1--3, later documented entry in months 4--10, no prenatal care, and
unknown start timing. The early candidate set contains months 1--3. The
documented-start set contains months 1--10. The all-record set adds no-care and
unknown-timing records and therefore bounds candidate and event availability
under retrospective complete scoring. The care-month rule operationalizes
documented entry into prenatal care rather than feature-level EHR availability;
the birth-certificate model serves as a shared retrospective ranking instrument.

The baseline-oriented score uses nine maternal birth-certificate variables:
race, height, pre-pregnancy BMI and weight, previous live births now living,
previous live births now dead, other pregnancy outcomes, pre-pregnancy
diabetes, and pre-pregnancy hypertension. Outcome, delivery, and neonatal
fields are excluded. Numeric variables use development-set median imputation
and standardization; race uses most-frequent imputation and one-hot encoding.
One LightGBM model per outcome uses 350 trees, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves,
minimum child size 100, and seed 2026 \citep{ke2017lightgbm}. The audit compares
candidate and budget policies on a shared score vector while holding the model
family constant.

\subsection{Decision metrics}

Let $\mathcal P$ contain $N$ records and $E=\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}Y_i$ events.
For candidate set $S$, event count $E_S$, and integer budget $b$, deterministic
tie breaking defines the $b$ highest scores $\topset(S,b)$. Their true-positive
count is
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{TP}(S,b)=\sum_{i\in\topset(S,b)}Y_i.
  \label{eq:tp}
\end{equation}
We report both within-gate performance and population reach:
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{Precision}(S,b)&=\frac{\operatorname{TP}(S,b)}{b}, &
  \receligible(S,b)&=\frac{\operatorname{TP}(S,b)}{E_S},
  \label{eq:within_metrics}\\
  \capture(S,b)&=\frac{\operatorname{TP}(S,b)}{E}, &
  \ceiling(S)&=\frac{E_S}{E}.
  \label{eq:population_metrics}
\end{align}
Population capture factors into retrieval and reach,
\begin{equation}
  \capture(S,b)=\receligible(S,b)\,\ceiling(S).
  \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
Each result therefore reports target-population $N$ and $E$, eligible $n$ and
$E_S$, exact selected $n$, precision, eligible recall, population capture, and
the event ceiling. ROC-AUC and average precision (AP) provide a ranking-signal
check; the four-cell contrasts carry the primary decision claim.

\subsection{Four-cell eligibility--capacity audit}

Let $S_e\subseteq S_a$ be early and all-record candidate sets. A nominal
fraction $q$ induces $b_e=\operatorname{round}(q|S_e|)$ and
$b_a=\operatorname{round}(q|S_a|)$. With $b_a\leq |S_e|$, the complete design is
\begin{equation}
  C_{uv}=\capture(S_u,b_v),\qquad u,v\in\{0,1\},
  \label{eq:fourcell}
\end{equation}
where 0 denotes early and 1 denotes all-record eligibility or capacity. The
usual cohort-relative contrast is $\Delta=C_{11}-C_{00}$. Its interaction is
$I=C_{11}-C_{10}-C_{01}+C_{00}$. We retain both fixed-budget eligibility
contrasts and summarize the joint change with the standard symmetric Shapley
allocation \citep{shapley1953value}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \phi_E&=\tfrac12\big[(C_{10}-C_{00})+(C_{11}-C_{01})\big],\\
  \phi_B&=\tfrac12\big[(C_{01}-C_{00})+(C_{11}-C_{10})\big].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shapley}
\end{equation}
The exact identity $\Delta=\phi_E+\phi_B$ holds, and
$s_B=\phi_B/\Delta$ gives the symmetric capacity share for positive $\Delta$.
For a frozen deterministic ranking, increasing $b$ adds nested positions, so
the capacity component is non-negative; expanding $S$ at fixed $b$ can have
either sign. A uniformly random ranking supplies the mechanical reference
$\operatorname{E}[\capture(S,b)]=(b/|S|)(E_S/E)$.

\subsection{Evaluation and mechanism stress test}

The empirical audit uses $q\in\{0.05,0.10\}$. Point estimates are accompanied
by 500 paired full-size empirical resamples per outcome and $q$. Each resample
uses common record multiplicities across all four cells while holding the
observed scores and absolute budgets fixed. The resulting central 95\% ranges
measure conditional stability of the reported four-cell contrasts.

The controlled simulation crosses availability fractions 0.5, 0.7, and 0.9;
risk--availability relationships in which higher risk is less available,
independent, or more available; score noise levels 0.5, 1, and 2; and
$q\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$. These factors yield 81 conditions. Each of 100
repetitions contains 100,000 evaluation records and an independent 50,000-record
threshold-selection population. Outcomes, availability, and scores are shared
across the compared policies within a repetition.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\FloatBarrier

\subsection{Candidate eligibility and event ceilings}

Early entry contains about 74.7\% of target-population records, but only 71.8\%
of preterm events, 70.6\% of NICU events, and 69.4\% of low-birth-weight events.
Before ranking begins, the early gate therefore leaves 28.2--30.6\% of events
outside its reachable population. Including every documented prenatal-care
start in months 1--10 raises the event ceiling to 91.1--92.1\%.

In absolute terms, early eligibility excludes 74,624--89,111 outcome events
across the three tasks. Even the documented-start gate leaves 20,558--27,087
events outside the candidate population. These are reach constraints: changing
the ranking leaves events outside its decision set unreachable.

The excluded records carry concentrated risk. Outcome prevalence among no-care
records is 2.4--2.7 times the early-entry prevalence across the three targets;
unknown-timing records are 1.7--1.9 times higher. Candidate expansion therefore
changes both population reach and case mix, making a full-population event
denominator essential.

\subsection{Eligibility--capacity accounting under \texorpdfstring{top-$q$}{top-q} selection}

Figure~\ref{fig:headline} and Table~\ref{tab:main_results} connect the nominal
operating point to its actual workload. Across outcomes, the selected count
increases from approximately 260,000 to 348,000. Population event capture rises
from 14.63\% to 20.30\% for preterm birth, 13.93\% to 19.52\% for NICU, and
16.06\% to 22.83\% for low birth weight. These are relative gains of
38.8\%, 40.1\%, and 42.2\%, respectively.

The proportional policy adds 87,931--88,023 service slots per outcome and
captures 16,505--17,177 additional events. Its marginal yield is therefore
18.8--19.5 additional events per 100 added slots, equivalent to 5.1--5.3
additional selections per extra captured event. Thus, the higher capture is
obtained under an explicitly 33.8\% larger service capacity.

\input{tables/table1_shapley_q10.tex}

The four-cell audit changes the interpretation of those gains. Symmetric
accounting assigns 2.11--2.60 percentage points to eligibility and
3.47--4.17 points to additional capacity, placing 61.6--62.2\% of the joint
contrast on the workload increase. Holding the early budget fixed yields
eligibility gains of 1.89--2.27 points; holding the all-record budget fixed
yields 2.32--2.93 points. Both are materially smaller than the joint
5.59--6.77-point cohort-relative contrast.

The pattern persists at $q=5\%$, where capacity receives 63.9--65.6\% of the
symmetric allocation. Conditional ranges remain above 60.4\% for every target
and operating point. A random ranking allocates 79.7--87.9\% to capacity at
$q=10\%$, showing that a majority capacity allocation can arise mechanically
under a proportional budget. The learned ranking shifts more of
the joint gain toward eligibility because the added candidate groups contain
greater event mass. Complete four-cell counts and both operating points appear
in Appendix~\ref{app:shapley}.

\subsection{Fixed-budget and transported-threshold policies}

Table~\ref{tab:policy_contrasts} compares three policies on the same frozen
score vector. Cohort-relative top-10\% evaluation jointly changes eligibility
and workload. Fixing the all-record absolute budget isolates candidate
expansion: capture increases by 2.32--2.93 points, with exactly the same number
selected on both sides. A threshold chosen once on the CDC 2022 validation
split selects 9.15--9.82\% of early-entry records and 10.20--10.40\% of all
records in CDC 2023. Under that transported threshold, the all-record worklist
is 1.42--1.49 times larger and capture increases by 6.36--8.19 points.

\input{tables/table_policy_contrasts_q10.tex}

These contrasts differ because they implement different resource commitments
on a shared ranking. A complete cross-cohort claim therefore states its
candidate rule, selected count, and event denominator alongside the gain.

The cross-year workload check points in the same direction. Transporting the
stored CDC 2022 top-10\% score cutoffs to target-specific CDC 2024 cohorts
selected 10.65\% of preterm records, 9.50\% of NICU records, and 8.30\% of
low-birth-weight records. Relative to a 10\% service budget, these rates create
22,668 additional preterm slots but leave 17,621 NICU and 59,716
low-birth-weight slots unfilled. A fixed score cutoff therefore defines a
workload that can move across years; the exact selected count remains the
operational quantity for capacity planning.

\subsection{Controlled mechanism stress test}

Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic} maps the protocol difference across all 81 synthetic
conditions. Eighteen conditions report a positive cohort-relative gain while
candidate expansion at the all-record absolute budget reduces population
capture. Nine have a central replicate range entirely below zero. Every
reversal arises when higher-risk records are preferentially available, so the
newly admitted pool is lower risk and can displace selected events.

At 70\% availability, medium score quality, and $q=10\%$, for example,
cohort-relative capture increases 5.52 points while fixed-budget candidate
expansion changes capture by $-0.28$ points; 93 of 100 repetitions reverse.
The largest mean absolute interaction is 9.49 points. In CDC 2023, all six
fixed-budget eligibility contrasts remain positive, so the ambiguity changes
effect magnitude. The controlled grid shows that selective availability can
also change effect direction.

\subsection{Temporal score diagnostics}

The four-cell accounting fixes one CDC 2023 score vector, isolating its
within-year policy contrasts from score drift. A temporal check additionally
shows that the frozen 2022 pipelines retain similar aggregate discrimination in
CDC 2023 and provide a transported ranking for the policy audit.
Across outcomes, ROC-AUC changes by at most 0.003 and average precision by at
most 0.003 from the CDC 2022 validation split to CDC 2023. CDC 2023 Brier skill
relative to a prevalence-only forecast is 1.7--2.4\%. These diagnostics anchor
the empirical audit in transported scores while preserving the decision policy
as the primary estimand.

\subsection{Sensitivity and robustness}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

Two feature ablations preserve the allocation pattern. Removing maternal race
and refitting each outcome model at $q=10\%$ leaves capacity shares of
60.9--62.4\%. A separate eight-feature specification removes prior other
pregnancy outcomes (\texttt{prior\_terminations}) and repeats the complete audit
at both budgets with paired resampling. Capacity still accounts for
61.5--64.3\% of the joint contrast, and all interaction ranges remain above
zero (+0.32 to +0.69 percentage points). The result therefore persists across
two alternative conservative landmark specifications.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Resource interpretation of top-$q$ evaluation.}
Top-$q$ evaluation is operationally coherent when service volume scales with
the candidate population; a fixed absolute budget answers the constrained
capacity question. The distinction is material here: the headline
38.8--42.2\% relative capture gain includes a 33.8\% workload increase. The
four-cell design makes that resource policy visible and supplies both
fixed-budget contrasts.

\paragraph{Separating reach, ranking, and capacity.}
The candidate gate determines the event ceiling, ranking determines which
eligible cases approach the cutoff, and capacity determines how many positions
can be filled. Freezing the score vector isolates broader reach at fixed
capacity from proportional resource expansion; the interaction records when
the value of one lever depends on the other. Transported score cutoffs form a
third regime in which workload emerges from the subsequent score distribution,
as the CDC 2024 check illustrates.

\paragraph{Applicability across deterministic score vectors.}
The four-cell accounting accepts any fixed deterministic score vector, and capacity monotonicity
follows from the nesting of top-$b$ selections independently of the LightGBM
functional form. Candidate expansion at fixed $b$ depends on which scores and
events enter the pool, so its sign remains empirical. The random-ranking
reference and the controlled grid spanning three score-noise levels recover
both properties: capacity usually explains most of a proportional-budget
contrast, while selective availability can reverse the fixed-budget effect.
Model choice changes where a study lies in this mechanism map; the audit
defines the decision comparison around it.

\paragraph{A recommended reporting profile.}
Based on the empirical and controlled audits, Table~\ref{tab:reporting_profile}
proposes a minimum reporting checklist for cross-cohort top-$q$ comparisons.
The checklist links each reported number to the target population, candidate
gate, resource rule, or decomposition needed to interpret the operating point.

The profile applies wherever a score becomes a finite worklist: imaging queues,
screening, case management, and public-sector allocation all require a declared
fixed, proportional, or threshold-driven capacity rule. Exact selected counts
prevent a stable percentage from disguising a changing workload.

\paragraph{Implementation in existing evaluation pipelines.}
Stored identifiers, outcomes, candidate masks, and one score vector are enough
to construct the four cells. Implementations record both budgets, selected
events, and the full-population denominator, then reuse common record weights
for paired resampling. Aggregate counts and configurations provide an auditable
path from a top-$q$ result to its service policy without releasing row-level
clinical data.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Top-fraction comparisons across cohorts encode service decisions in addition
to ranking summaries. In the CDC audit, the same nominal top 10\% expands the
workload by one third, and symmetric accounting assigns about 62\% of the
capture gain to that capacity increase. Controlled experiments identify
conditions in which cohort-relative and fixed-budget conclusions have opposite
signs. Reporting the target population, candidate rule, exact selected count,
full-population event denominator, and fixed-budget contrast makes the policy
claim auditable. The present scope is a birth-certificate score and a
care-entry eligibility proxy; prospective EHR landmarks, subgroup budgets, and
additional temporal cohorts provide the next transport tests.

\section{Synthetic Grid Details}
\label{app:synthetic}

The synthetic experiment crosses three availability fractions, three
risk--availability slopes, three score-noise levels, and three selection
fractions. Intercepts are solved numerically so the target marginal prevalence
or availability fraction holds before Monte Carlo variation. Common random
numbers are used across all 81 conditions within each repetition, and the
available-subset and all-population policies share outcomes and score vectors.
Reported 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles describe the replicate distribution.

The score-noise levels $\sigma_R=0.5,1,2$ imply expected correlations between
score and latent risk of approximately 0.894, 0.707, and 0.447. The largest
absolute replicate-level interaction is 10.72 percentage points. Full
condition summaries and replicate outputs are retained in the prepared release
bundle.

\section{Audit Identities and Artifacts}
\label{app:reproducibility}

\paragraph{Deterministic identity checks.}
For fixed $S$, scores, and deterministic tie breaking,
$\topset(S,b_e)\subseteq\topset(S,b_a)$ whenever $b_e\leq b_a$. Increasing the
budget adds exactly $b_a-b_e$ ranked positions and between zero and
$b_a-b_e$ events. Both marginal capacity contrasts are therefore non-negative,
as is their average $\phi_B$. Substituting Equation~\ref{eq:shapley} also gives
$\phi_E+\phi_B=C_{11}-C_{00}$ exactly. These statements concern a shared score
vector; retraining introduces an additional factor.

\paragraph{Artifacts.}

All manuscript figures and tables are generated from included CSV files.
Verification scripts assert the six primary Shapley rows, 500 paired empirical
resamples per target and budget, exact accounting identities, all 81 synthetic
conditions, 18 mean sign reversals, and 9 conditions whose central replicate
range is negative. The prepared release bundle contains aggregate inputs,
figure and table generators, verification scripts, the locked synthetic
configuration and runner, synthetic replicate outputs, and the temporal
threshold-transport script. It redistributes no individual-level Natality
records. Use of the public-use files follows the NCHS Data User Agreement
\citep{cdcDataAgreement}; LightGBM is distributed under the MIT License
\citep{lightgbmRepo}.

\paragraph{Raw-data reconstruction.}
The CDC 2022 split is drawn before target-specific exclusions with
\texttt{default\_rng(2026)}: 20\% enters validation and the remainder enters
training, on which preprocessing is fitted. Unknown outcomes are excluded
target-wise, and original row order breaks score ties. Thresholds are selected
on the target-specific CDC 2022 all-record validation pool and applied with
$\geq$. Empirical and synthetic seeds are 2027 and 2028. Section~3 and
Appendices~A--C specify the remaining cohort, model, and estimand details.
